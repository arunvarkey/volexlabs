Two tools, one chain

Anvil proves it. Forge draws it. They are separate engines doing separate jobs — a Eurocode checker is not a geometry kernel — and the seam between them is where the value is: a steel connection designed to EN 1993-1-8 in Anvil becomes a plate with holes in it in Forge, without being re-drawn by hand.

ANVIL — CALCULATION

Code checks with full derivations

Steel, concrete, timber, masonry, composite, geotechnical and seismic design. Each check prints demand, capacity, utilisation, the clause it came from and the step-by-step working behind it.

FORGE — GEOMETRY

Text to CAD, with an FEA cross-check

Describe a part; get a solid model and STEP, STL, DXF or 3MF. A finite element pass reports where the model works hardest — as a second opinion on the hand calculation, never as a substitute for it.

EVIDENCE

Validation you can open

Every calculation type carries a badge linking to the published worked examples behind it — the source, the expected value, and what this build actually produced. Re-run live, not a stored claim.

For mechanical engineers

Forge is the mechanical half, and it is held to the same standard as the structural half: it reports what it computed, names the table it came from, and says plainly what it did not check.

FITS & TOLERANCES

ISO 286 limits and fits, computed

Name a fit in the prompt — H7/h6, H8/f7 — and the limits, the clearance or interference and the resulting shaft and hole sizes are computed from the published ISO 286 tables and printed with the part. Checkable against the table, not against a claim.

MANUFACTURABILITY

DFM notes before the quote

Wall thickness, minimum feature size and machinability are reported against the process the part is headed for — CNC, sheet, print — so a part that cannot be made comes back as a note rather than as a rejected quote from a fabricator.

CROSS-CHECK

A von Mises pass, honestly labelled

The finite element pass shows where the geometry works hardest. It returns no rating, no safety factor and no pass or fail, because a linear-elastic peak at a bolt hole reaches yield on connections that are fully compliant. It is a second opinion, and it is labelled as one.

How a job runs

  1. State the problemPick a calculation type and enter the geometry, materials and actions — or describe it in plain text and let the parser fill the form for you to check.
  2. Read the checks, not just the verdictEvery check shows demand against capacity with its clause. The governing one is marked. Warnings appear where a result is acceptable but worth a second look.
  3. Follow the derivationEach result expands into its steps: the symbol, the formula, the substituted numbers and the clause. Nothing is asserted without its working.
  4. Cross-check the geometry (optional)Send the proven design to Forge for a solid model, its ISO 286 fits and a finite element pass. Forge reports where the model works hardest; it returns no rating and no pass/fail, because a linear-elastic peak at a bolt hole reaches yield on connections that are fully compliant.
  5. Export for sign-offA clause-referenced PDF with inputs, checks, derivations, the verdict and a stated scope-and-limitations block. Every calculation is version-stamped, so any result can be reproduced on any later date.

Verification and limits

Volex is checked against published worked examples. Each case names its source, its tolerance band, the expected value and what the current build produced — and re-runs when you open the page rather than reporting a stored number. Open any calculation and click its validation badge to read the cases behind it.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT DO

  • It does not replace independent checking by a chartered engineer, nor professional indemnity sign-off. The engineer issuing a calculation remains responsible for it.
  • Only the checks printed in a result have been performed. The absence of a check is not a statement that it is satisfied.
  • Inputs, load cases, combinations, support conditions and material grades are as supplied by the user and are not independently verified.
  • Agreement with a published worked example is evidence of agreement with that example. It is not proof of correctness for your design.
  • Forge's finite element pass is a second opinion on geometry. It returns no rating, no safety factor and no pass/fail, and must not be presented as a strength check.
  • ISO 286 fits named in a prompt are computed. A full tolerance stack-up across a chain of mating parts is not performed.
  • No geometric tolerancing. True position, flatness, perpendicularity and runout are not applied, checked or exported as GD&T.
  • No mould-flow, draft or sink-mark analysis. Manufacturability notes cover printability and machinability only.
  • Standard-part geometry — T-slot nuts, eye bolts and the like — is modelled to the nominal dimensions given, not built from certified tables, and carries no load rating.
